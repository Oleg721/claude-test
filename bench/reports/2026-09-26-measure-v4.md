Haiku and Sonnet: 8 of 8 calls exact (315 / 157), identical picks every time; Sonnet's three apple answers are byte-identical (260 output tokens). Opus: the banana exact, the apple 0 of 3 — it set `maskFitsItem` false each time and the server fell back to its visual guess. The visual guesses themselves: 160–190 g for the 314 g apple, 120–130 g for the 157 g banana — the v3 failure mode, now recorded in a field of its own instead of the answer.

Opus's reading of the apple mask is geometrically right and practically wrong: the footprint runs 10.2 × 8.8 cm around an 8.5 cm apple, so the length is 20 % over, but the area (59.6 cm²) is only 5 % over an 8.5 cm circle — the excess is a thin shadow fringe with no height, which the calibrated 0.87 already absorbs. The gate as worded ("runs wider than the item") lets a fringe count as a failure. A v5 candidate: define false as a gross overrun (half again the item or more) or a raised neighbour in the mask, describe the shadow fringe as normal, and say that on a round footprint the mean width is about 0.7 × the width.
